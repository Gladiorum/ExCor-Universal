# Joint discovery and empirical cognitive architecture evaluation

**Status:** Experimental research proposals; not validated, mandatory, or an amendment to the Universal Core.
**Date:** 2026-11-27
**Provenance:** Two separately commissioned AI research investigations (independent architecture audit and joint-discovery follow-up), critically reviewed by the originating ExCor instance. Shared models, platform, evidence and commissioning can limit independence. Relative compute and model superiority are not established. This is a depersonalised synthesis, not the full private reports. The cited literature was identified in those reports; this synthesis does not independently replicate the studies.

## Purpose

Discover whether human–AI interaction can generate useful distinctions, new objectives and transferable capabilities that are less accessible to either participant working separately within comparable resources. Investigate the methods rather than presume their superiority. These candidates operationalise existing principles of catalytic cognitive augmentation, recursive capability acquisition, adaptive operational philosophy and cognitive orchestration.

## 1. Challenge shared equivalence

Agreement may conceal a common blind spot. Where two examples satisfy both participants' descriptions but yield materially different outcomes:

1. Record the initial descriptions independently when feasible.
2. Construct or observe contrasting cases that preserve the shared description.
3. Ask the participant with relevant observational or practical access to demonstrate the difference.
4. Generate competing rules explaining the difference and the smallest new case on which they disagree.
5. Try inverse construction: deliberately reproduce or reverse the effect in a fresh case.
6. Record unresolved alternatives, costs, failures and whether the distinction was already known.

Example: two edits can have identical sound onset and reaction timing while differing in when a viewer identifies the sound's source. The initial instruction does not distinguish them. Test recognition with unfamiliar viewers; do not infer the effect solely from the edit timeline. This is an illustrative example, not an experimental result.

Shared agreement is one promising search location, not an empirically established optimum. Retain contradiction analysis, interdisciplinary analogy, open exploration and other discovery methods.

## 2. Lossy reconstruction and new problem formation

Translate a draft or artefact into a deliberately incomplete representation. Have another participant reconstruct a different artefact from that representation. Inspect unexpected properties, then ask what new purpose would make an unexpected property essential. Attempt a construction that uses it. Compare against ordinary collaborative sketching or equally varied perturbations before attributing gains to controlled representational loss.

This can generate a new brief rather than merely optimise the original brief. Do not require a predetermined outcome during exploration.

## 3. Capability inheritance

When acquiring a useful capability, preserve a reproducible successful operation, a nearby failure and a way to vary the distinction. Ask whether using that capability creates the conditions for acquiring its successor.

Test whether a fresh participant or host given the manipulable contrast can acquire a subsequent capability more efficiently than one given only a verbal lesson or the original material. Include construction, retrieval and maintenance costs. Distinguish operator skill, transferable records and capabilities dependent on the particular human–AI pairing. Allow inherited methods to be discarded when they constrain discovery.

## 4. Discriminating success cases

When different methods succeed on the same case, construct a minimal variation on which their next actions or predictions differ. If both fail, use the failure to derive a challenger. Test on a naturally occurring or independently selected case to reduce evaluator gaming.

Preserve cases that distinguish methods, not merely successful outputs. Evaluate both a new method and the process by which it was discovered. Compare method discovery against ordinary retrospective review under equal search budgets.

## 5. Dynamic cognitive resource allocation

Route consequential unresolved questions according to observational access, competence, discriminating evidence, cost, urgency and reversibility rather than presumed model hierarchy. A measurement, operator demonstration, domain expert, additional model or ordinary extended reasoning may be preferable in different circumstances. Compare against the strongest practical simpler alternative; do not assign a permanent model tier without evidence.

When appropriate, record the unresolved decision, plausible alternatives, the observation that could change the choice, the means of obtaining it, a prospective prediction and the actual result.

## 6. Empirical evaluation

Exploration: preserve contrasting examples, unexpected questions and failed descriptions. Allow goals and vocabulary to change.

Development: investigate prior art, competing explanations and counterexamples; attempt reproduction, reversal and transfer.

Confirmation: freeze the claim, comparator, resource budget, scoring and regression criteria before exposing a fresh case. Distinguish novelty to the operator, novelty to a workflow and field-wide novelty. Distinguish articulation of existing tacit knowledge, exposure to an idea, transfer and evidence of interaction-generated conceptual content. No finite trial establishes that a participant could never discover an idea independently.

Candidate comparisons:
- Ordinary assistance versus short, flat and full ExCor instructions on concealed, independently resolved tasks.
- One model versus repeated sampling, different models or direct measurement under comparable total budgets.
- Adaptive human–AI exchange versus independent work followed by one-time pooling or nonadaptive replay.
- Inherited contrast versus verbal lesson versus raw materials.
- Assisted outcomes versus delayed unaided transfer; distinguish useful delegation from unwanted dependency.
- Strategic intelligence alerts versus a manual scan; sample omissions, deduplicate and report costs and missed opportunities within the monitored source boundary.
- Migration with stale, missing, superseded or conflicting authority records; test evidence retrieval, approval scope and disclosure constraints.

Report costs, serious errors, abandoned cases, unresolved outcomes and uncertainty. Subjective creative value and delayed conceptual breakthroughs may be retained prospectively without claiming immediate empirical confirmation. Stop evaluation when its burden outweighs its likely benefit. Promote, narrow or retire a method only through appropriate evidence and operator approval.

## 7. Minimal practical pilot

Choose one real, low-risk task with an observable contrast or a useful artefact. Preserve the initial descriptions and a nearby counterexample. Try the least expensive discriminating observation, then attempt a fresh construction. Record what changed, whether it worked and whether the result was already known. Reuse ordinary work where possible; no elaborate reporting infrastructure is required.

Neither audit ran controlled comparative experiments. No evidence here establishes general model superiority, emergent intelligence, autonomous recursive improvement or a demonstrated ExCor performance gain.
